Lee 1988 tail, identifiability: The Lee 1988 blockade tail (30% of the 1 h value at 168 h after 50 mg) can be explained either by slow MOR dissociation (the stage-J explanation: a shared koff scale) or by a long terminal plasma phase of naltrexone/6-beta-naltrexol. The adopted two-compartment PK has a 9.8 h terminal phase, well short of the apparent 96 h half-life Verebey 1976 reports from 24 to 72 h in its abstract (held out of the primary fit as pk_summary_sensitivity_terminal.csv, since the abstract does not say which dose it follows). Slow dissociation pushes steady-state occupancy at 1.5-6 mg UP: receptor-bound drug carries over between daily doses, so occupancy accumulates and the peak-trough swing flattens. A long terminal PK phase pushes it DOWN, towards the rapid-equilibrium (A2-like) values: the tail is then made by low persisting concentrations that occupy receptors after 50 mg but are 10-30 times lower after 1.5-6 mg, so they add little occupancy at low doses. The terminal-phase sensitivity fit (R1 fix round 3) is the one direct test of this in these data, and it PARTLY separates them: given the 24-72 h values the PK reaches a 23 h terminal phase, the fitted koff scale becomes about 1.7 times less extreme, the PET fit improves, and occupancy at 4.5 mg falls by about 10 percentage points -- the predicted direction. It is not decisive, because that PK costs a large increase in weighted SSE against the reported 10 h half-lives (which are a 0-24 h biexponential estimand, not the model's terminal slope), so the data are internally inconsistent on this point. Slow dissociation still carries most of the tail under every analysis: koff_NTX stays roughly 290-510 times below the in vitro value. Occupancy at 4.5 mg should therefore be read across both analyses, not from the primary alone.
